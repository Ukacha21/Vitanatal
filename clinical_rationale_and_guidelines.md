## 1. The Cascade of Interventions: How Avoidable C-Sections Happen

The vast majority of unexpected cesarean deliveries are not caused by acute anatomical emergency, but by a well-documented clinical cascade known in obstetric literature as the **"Cascade of Medical Interventions"**:

```
Maternal Anxiety / Latent Phase Discomfort
                   │
                   ▼
Premature Hospital Admission (Cervix < 3 cm dilated)
                   │
                   ▼
Confinement to Hospital Bed & Continuous Rigid CTG Belt
                   │
                   ▼
Artificial Rupture of Membranes (AROM) & Synthetic Oxytocin (Pitocin)
                   │
                   ▼
Uterine Tachysystole (Excessive Contractions) & Lack of Rest Between Waves
                   │
                   ▼
Transient Fetal Heart Rate Decelerations (Fetal Distress Alarm)
                   │
                   ▼
🚨 EMERGENCY CESAREAN SECTION SURGERY
```

### Breaking the Cascade with Project No-Sezaryen
1. **Accurate Latent vs. Active Labor Identification (EHG Spectral Analysis):** The mother remains comfortably in her home environment until active labor is genuinely established (cervix dilating, synchronized uterine bursts). Delaying admission until $\ge 5 \text{ cm}$ dilation reduces the risk of cesarean surgery by over **50%**.
2. **Freedom of Maternal Movement:** Unlike rigid clinic CTG belts that bind the mother to a bed, the wireless **PartusBand** allows active upright movement, walking, and birthing ball rocking—shortening labor by an average of 1.5 hours and promoting natural pelvic descent.

## 3. FIGO & ACOG Cardiotocography (CTG) Non-Stress Test Standards

The device’s automated Non-Stress Test (NST) scoring conforms to **International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics (FIGO)** standards:

### Physiological Benchmarks
1. **Baseline Fetal Heart Rate (FHR):**
   - Normal: $110 \text{ bpm} – 160 \text{ bpm}$
   - Bradycardia: $< 110 \text{ bpm}$ (sustained $>3 \text{ min}$ triggers emergency escalation)
   - Tachycardia: $> 160 \text{ bpm}$ (sustained $>10 \text{ min}$ flags potential maternal fever or fetal infection)
2. **Baseline Variability:**
   - Normal (Moderate): $5 \text{ bpm} – 25 \text{ bpm}$ (strong indicator of an oxygenated, intact autonomic nervous system)
   - Silent / Minimal: $< 5 \text{ bpm}$ (flags fetal sleep cycle or hypoxia if prolonged)
   - Marked: $> 25 \text{ bpm}$
3. **Accelerations:**
   - Reactive / Healthy: At least $2$ accelerations of $\ge 15 \text{ bpm}$ above baseline lasting $\ge 15 \text{ seconds}$ in a 20-minute recording window.
4. **Decelerations:**
   - Early Decelerations: Synchronous with contractions $\rightarrow$ Benign fetal head compression.
   - Late Decelerations: Lagging after contraction peak $\rightarrow$ Uteroplacental insufficiency (Urgent clinical alert).
   - Variable Decelerations: Sharp drops $\rightarrow$ Umbilical cord compression.
